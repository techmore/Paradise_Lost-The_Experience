The Poem

An Epic of Unparalleled Ambition

Of Man's First Disobedience, and the Fruit of that Forbidden Tree… So begins John Milton's monumental epic, first published in 1667. Composed after Milton had gone completely blind, the poem was dictated in its entirety to amanuenses.

Spanning ten books, Paradise Lost ranges across Heaven, Hell, and the newly created Earth. It recounts the Fall of Satan and his rebel angels, the creation of the world, and humanity's temptation and exile from Eden—all while seeking to "justify the ways of God to men."

Milton's blank verse transformed English poetry. His Satan remains one of literature's most compelling antiheroes, his Eve a figure of profound complexity, and his vision of cosmic history a landmark of the Western imagination.
