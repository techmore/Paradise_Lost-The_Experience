Curated Resources

Everything you need to study, enjoy, and deepen your understanding of Paradise Lost—all freely accessible online.

Structured Courses

Guided, self-paced learning experiences

Hillsdale College — Paradise Lost

9 video lectures (~40 min each) by Prof. Stephen Smith. Book-by-book walkthrough from Hell through the Fall to Redemption. Strong on literary beauty, Satan's rhetoric, human nature, and providence. Includes study guides and quizzes for a free certificate.

Course Video

DartmouthX: John Milton: Paradise Lost (edX)

4-week self-paced course by Prof. Thomas Luxon. Short video lectures, reading assignments, and social annotation using the excellent Milton Reading Room. May be archived but materials often still viewable.

Course Video

University Lectures

Deep academic dives from leading scholars

Open Yale — ENGL 220: Milton (Prof. John Rogers)

Full course with excellent close readings. Key Paradise Lost lectures include Book I (Lecture 9), Book III (13), Books V–VI (15), Books VII–VIII (16), Book IX (17–18), and Books XI–XII (19–20). Videos, transcripts, and syllabus all freely available.

Course Video

Benjamin McEvoy — How to Read Paradise Lost

A practical, enthusiastic introduction to the poem. Highly recommended as a first listen before diving into the text. Hundreds of thousands of views on YouTube.

Video

Richard Strier (UChicago) — Paradise Lost Lectures

The University of Chicago scholar's incisive lectures on Paradise Lost, available on YouTube. Known for sharp close readings and historical context.

Course Hero — Paradise Lost Summaries

Book-by-book video summaries covering character, plot, and key themes. Useful for review and orientation before reading each book.

Suggested Study Paths

Four approaches depending on your goals and available time

Beginner / Faith-Focused Path

Best if you want a guided, theologically-grounded introduction.

  1. Hillsdale course (9 video lectures) — provides structure and context
  2. Follow along in the Milton Reading Room with annotations
  3. Listen to an audiobook narration alongside your reading

Analytical Deep Dive

Best if you want to engage critically with the poem.

  1. Milton Reading Room as your primary text
  2. Selected Yale lectures by book (start with Books I, IV, IX)
  3. Benjamin McEvoy's intro for orientation
  4. Track biblical parallels (Genesis, Job, Revelation) in your notes

Slow Reading Path

One book per week. Savor the poem over a season.

  1. Read one book per week using the Reading Room annotations
  2. Watch one lecture per book (Hillsdale or Yale)
  3. Journal on themes: free will, temptation, redemption, hierarchy

Full Immersion

Combine a course, audiobook, and personal reflection.

  1. Listen to the audiobook during commutes for fluency
  2. Work through Hillsdale or Yale course systematically
  3. Read with Reading Room annotations for depth
  4. Take thematic notes connecting to providence, virtue, and the Rule of Life
